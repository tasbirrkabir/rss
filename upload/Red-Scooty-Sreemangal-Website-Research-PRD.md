# RED SCOOTY SREEMANGAL — Website Research, Product Requirements & UX/UI Specification

**Document type:** Research + PRD + UI/UX + CRO + Development Specification  
**Prepared for:** Tasbir Kabir  
**Research objective:** Build a premium demo/prototype for Red Scooty Sreemangal and use it as a partnership / website acquisition asset.  
**Research date:** 22 September 2026  
**Primary market:** Sreemangal, Moulvibazar, Bangladesh  
**Primary conversion:** Booking enquiry / direct contact  
**Recommended demo scope:** One high-quality, mobile-first website; no complex live inventory in v1.

---

## 0. Executive Summary

Red Scooty Sreemangal is a local tourism-mobility brand focused on scooty rental for exploring Sreemangal. Its strongest public-facing proposition is experiential rather than purely transactional: the Facebook page describes the service around exploring tea gardens and nature, while an indexed Facebook post promoted a **৳2-per-minute** offer. The page currently shown in the supplied screenshot has approximately **3.1K followers**, a direct call CTA, the Jalalia Road location, and a Gmail contact. Search results also surface the public handle `@redscootysmg` and an Instagram Reel associated with the same brand.

The strongest strategic signal is that this is not a generic vehicle-rental lead. Red Scooty has a destination-specific brand: **the product is access to Sreemangal itself**. The website should therefore behave like a hybrid of:

1. a rental storefront,
2. a mini travel guide,
3. a trust layer,
4. and a frictionless booking funnel.

A successful demo should not feel like a conventional Bangladeshi local-business website. The recommended direction is **premium tourism editorial design with controlled/mid brutalism**: strong typography, red/black/off-white palette, oversized type, hard borders, subtle offset shadows, asymmetric compositions, real photography, and highly visible booking actions.

The key product decision is to avoid overbuilding before the business workflow is known. The demo should prove the concept with a **booking request flow**, not pretend to have real-time fleet availability unless Red Scooty confirms it.

# 4. Business Model Hypothesis

The public evidence suggests a straightforward rental business with tourism as the primary use case.

## Core value loop

```text
Tourist plans Sreemangal
        ↓
Looks for things to do / transport
        ↓
Finds Red Scooty through Facebook / Google / recommendations
        ↓
Needs price + requirements + location + availability
        ↓
Contacts Red Scooty
        ↓
Rental completed
        ↓
Tourist explores Sreemangal
        ↓
Photos / reels / word-of-mouth generate more demand
```

## Website opportunity

The website should remove uncertainty from the middle of this loop.

The most important information is likely:

- What can I rent?
- How much does it cost?
- Can I rent it on my date?
- What documents/requirements apply?
- Where do I collect it?
- Can I book before I arrive?
- How do I contact the business right now?

# 6. Destination Psychology

Sreemangal has a powerful product-market context for this brand.

Bangladesh government and tourism resources describe Sreemangal as a destination known for extensive tea gardens. Tourism resources also identify Lawachara National Park, Madhabpur Lake, Baikka Beel and other nearby attractions as major points of interest.

A current tourism guide lists Lawachara at roughly 8 km from town and Madhabpur Lake at roughly 20 km southeast of Sreemangal, while other public map/travel references provide different routing times and distances depending on the starting point.

### UX implication

The website should make the visitor feel that Red Scooty is the **enabler of the trip**, not an isolated rental shop.

The emotional journey should be:

**“I want to see Sreemangal.”**

→ **“I want freedom to move around.”**

→ **“Red Scooty can get me there.”**

→ **“I can request a ride now.”**

# 7. Target Users / Personas

## Persona A — Weekend Bangladeshi traveler

**Context:** 1–2 day trip with friend/partner.  
**Device:** Android mobile.  
**Discovery:** Facebook, TikTok/Reels, Google, friend recommendation.  
**Needs:** price, quick booking, location, route suggestions.  
**Fear:** hidden costs, wasting time, unclear rental rules.

### UX requirement

Show price/plan structure, pickup location, rules and one-tap booking quickly.

## Persona B — Couple traveler

**Context:** wants scenic, flexible exploration.  
**Need:** convenience + experience + visually appealing brand.  
**Decision trigger:** “Can we rent this easily and start exploring?”

### UX requirement

Destination imagery and route inspiration should sit close to rental CTA.

## Persona C — Young traveler/content creator

**Context:** wants unique locations and freedom.  
**Need:** Instagrammable routes, reels, quick mobility, social proof.

### UX requirement

Use strong photography and “where to ride” content to make the brand shareable.

## Persona D — Tourist planning before arrival

**Context:** not yet in Sreemangal.  
**Need:** trust and advance planning.  
**Decision trigger:** confidence that the rental is real, local and bookable.

### UX requirement

The site must expose Google Maps location, phone, booking flow, policies, social proof and clear business identity.

# 10. Typography System

## Recommended primary display font

**Bebas Neue**

Use for:

- hero headlines,
- giant section statements,
- product names,
- numerical highlights,
- editorial labels.

## Recommended body font

**DM Sans** or **Manrope**

Use for:

- body copy,
- navigation,
- forms,
- metadata,
- buttons,
- utility text.

## Typographic behavior

### Hero
`clamp(64px, 10vw, 150px)` for giant display headlines on desktop.

### Section headings
`clamp(48px, 7vw, 96px)`

### Body
16–18 px desktop / 15–16 px mobile.

### Microcopy
11–13 px, uppercase, tracking 0.08–0.14em.

### Typography rule

Use **contrast, not clutter**. One giant idea per section is preferable to several medium headlines.

# 12. UI Language

## Borders

Use 1–2 px black borders on cards and controls.

## Radius

- 0–6 px default
- 8–12 px only for selected media/functional elements
- Avoid universal 20–32 px rounded cards.

## Shadows

Prefer offset hard shadows:

`4px 4px 0 #111`

rather than soft SaaS shadows everywhere.

## Buttons

Use rectangular / slightly rounded controls with strong contrast.

Example:

`BOOK A SCOOTY →`

Black background, white label, red hover/pressed treatment or hard offset shadow.

## Images

Use large crops and intentional framing. Avoid generic stock photography wherever possible.

# 24. Conversion Psychology

## 24.1 Reduce uncertainty before asking for action

The visitor should see:

- what it is,
- where it is,
- what it costs or how pricing works,
- how booking works,
- what to bring,
- and how quickly the business responds.

## 24.2 Destination-first framing

People are not waking up thinking:

> “I want to rent a scooter.”

They are more likely to think:

> “I am going to Sreemangal. How do I explore it?”

The site therefore begins with **destination intent**, then introduces the rental as the natural solution.

## 24.3 Choice architecture

Do not present 12 options if the real fleet is small.

Present the few options that matter and make the decision obvious.

## 24.4 Action repetition

Primary CTA appears:

- hero,
- fleet,
- destination section,
- near end,
- sticky mobile bar.

Not every section needs its own competing CTA.

## 24.5 Scarcity

Use scarcity only if it is real.

Example:

`Only 2 scooters available` is prohibited unless live availability exists.

Instead use truthful urgency such as:

> Planning a weekend trip? Request early to confirm availability.

## 24.6 Social proof

Prefer proof that is visually native to the brand:

- actual rider photos,
- tagged reels,
- review screenshots,
- Google review links.

## 24.7 Momentum

The booking form should require minimal typing. Use select/dropdown/date controls wherever possible.
